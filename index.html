Section 1: The rise and fall

Goal scoring peaked in 1982, where are we now?

The overall scoring peaked in 1982 before a bumpy decline through 2021, the lowest scoring full-length season since the peak, averaging only 160 points per game. It has slowly risen since then. Then in 2026, the scoring soared to 179 points per game, its highest since 2013.

Of the 16 clubs playing in both decades of 1990s and 2020s, 15 now score less per game, and only Brisbane managed to score more. The biggest drop is North Melbourne from an average of (110 to 72) across the seasons. It is then followed by West Coast (96 to 68) and Essendon (103 to 77).

Section 2: Are teams kicking straighter?

Fewer shots taken, but not because of kicking

A goal is worth six points and a behind, which is a near miss, is worth one point. If goals decreased due to poor kicking, behinds would have risen showing that the kicks were near misses. However, they both fell together as a typical game went from 62 scoring shots in 1982 to 44 in 2021.

Accuracy rose for most of the 20th century, then levelled off. Between 1982 and 2021, it barely moved, going from 51.9% to 52.4%, while scoring shots fell by 18 a game. Scoring dropped because teams got fewer shots, not because they kicked worse.

Could the way clubs play be why?

To take a shot, players need space, and coaches have spent years finding ways to take that space away. One way is the flood, where a team pulls its forwards back to crowd the area the other team is attacking. The Western Bulldogs used a "Super-Flood" to beat an undefeated Essendon in 2000. Hawthorn's rolling zone, where players defend areas of the ground together, was a big part of its 2008 premiership. Collingwood then won in 2010 using a forward press, which traps the ball in its attacking half.

A University of South Australia study of the 1999 to 2019 seasons found that player density, tackles and stoppages went up while scoring went down. Teams were still getting the ball inside 50 about as often, which suggests defences had become harder to break through. Match results can't measure tactics directly, so this doesn't prove playing style is the reason, but the pattern does fit.

Section 3: Are games getting closer?

Winning margins shrank since the 1980s

As teams score less, it's harder for any team to win a game by a large margin. The high-scoring era was the 1980s and 1990s, where half of all games were decided by more than 32 points. In the 2020s, that figure has fallen to 26, and the share of games won by less than a goal has climbed from 9% in the 1980s to 14%.

Close game finishes are more common than ever. So far, 14.2% of games in the 2020s have been won by under a goal, the highest of any decade since 1900 and up from 9.0% in the 1980s and 9.5% in the 1990s Now half of the games are decided by 26 points or fewer as compared to 32 in the 1980s and 1990s.

Section 4: Teams

Seven clubs have topped the scoring since 2010

Scoring fell across the league, but some clubs still stood well above or below the rest, and the top spot keeps changing hands. Hawthorn led for four straight years from 2012 to 2015, and Sydney finished on top in 2026 with 110 points a game. The chart opens on Richmond, which ranked 2nd in 2018, 2019 and 2022, then last in each of the past three seasons.

Compared with the league average in the same season, the highest-scoring teams since 1990 were Geelong in 1992 (33 points a game above average), Hawthorn in 2012 and Essendon in 2000 (both 28 above). The lowest were Greater Western Sydney in its first season, 2012 (34 below), Melbourne in 2013 and 2014 (26 below), and Richmond in 2026 (25 below).

Section 5: Where the footy is played

Half the games, a quarter of the people

Victoria hosted 49% of the 2026 season's games with about 26% of Australia's population. The rest of the schedule sends clubs across the country: they made 184 trips between cities in 2026, and Adelaide–Melbourne was the busiest route. Scores vary by ground too: Perth Stadium has averaged the lowest score of the major venues since 2022.

AFL is still centred on Victoria, but it isn't only about population. South Australia hosted the most games for its size in 2026, at 1.67 per 100,000 residents, just ahead of Victoria at 1.50. New South Wales and Queensland, the first and third most populous states, hosted the fewest for their size, at 0.24 and 0.37. That means clubs have to travel: they made 184 trips between cities in 2026.

Section 6: Did the rule changes work?

Four rule changes, little lift. Then came 2026

The AFL keeps changing the rules. Scoring fell after the first interchange cap in 2014, rose a little when the cap was cut in 2016, fell again after the 6-6-6 starting positions in 2019, and barely moved after the stand rule in 2021. In 2026, the league brought in the last-disposal rule and a new centre ball-up, and enforced the stand rule more strictly. That season, the average rose by about 10 points, the biggest rise of the five.

Scoring changed by anywhere from 11 points down to 10 points up in the season after a rule change, so a new rule doesn't guarantee more goals. Three of the five changes were followed by lower scoring, and only 2016 and 2026 were followed by a rise. These comparisons can't prove the rules caused those changes, because other things change between seasons too.

So where did the goals go?

They went with the shots. Between 1982 and 2021 a typical game lost 18 scoring shots while kicking accuracy held steady, and margins narrowed along the way. The evidence points to playing style: more crowded, more defensive football that makes clean attacking chances harder to create. The 2026 season won back about five of those shots a game, but it still finished 46 points short of 1982. One strong season doesn't make a trend, so the next few will show whether the goals are really coming back.
